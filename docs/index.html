Methodological notes

1. Overview

NetZeroDB brings together top-down emissions pathways (national carbon budgets and global scenarios) and bottom-up corporate commitments (Science Based Targets initiative) so they can be compared on a common basis. The site is a static port of the project's Shiny application: all calculations run in your browser on pre-processed data exported from the R pipeline, and produce the same results as the Shiny app.

3. Processing principles

  • Each dataset is processed independently by its own script (R/sbti, R/uk_ccc, R/snbc, R/iea, R/nzdpu) and saved to data_prepared/. Raw inputs are never overwritten.
  • Data are stored in tidy, long format with explicit units. Original values and units are preserved; standardised fields (harmonised sector, scope labels, years) are added alongside.
  • SBTi cleaning: literal "NA" strings become missing; company names are trimmed; scope labels are standardised ("1.0" → 1, etc.); years given as FY2032 are parsed to integers. When a company has several commitment rows the best status is kept (Active > Target set > Extended > Removed).
  • Every script ends with validation checks (no missing keys, expected units, expected year ranges, target year ≥ base year). Values that look wrong are flagged, not altered — e.g. one intensity target with a reduction of 2,500 % is kept and labelled “outlier” in the table.
  • Units are never mixed silently. Where sources with different units appear on the same chart (IEA Mt CO2 vs. MtCO2e), a note is shown above the chart.

4. Sector harmonisation

Every source uses its own sector classification. A single mapping table (R/utils/sector_mapping.R) assigns each original sector to one of six cross-source harmonised sectors — Transport, Buildings, Agriculture, Industry, Energy, Waste — plus sectors that exist in only one source. UK CCC and SBTi sectors also map to a finer harmonised subsector; SNBC and IEA only exist at sector level. When values from several original sectors map to the same harmonised sector they are summed (UK CCC) or pooled (SBTi targets).

  • When a subsector is selected in the pathway view, SNBC and IEA are still drawn at sector level, dimmed, for reference.
  • If UK CCC has no matching subsector (e.g. SBTi’s Chemicals), the whole UK CCC sector is shown instead, dimmed, and a note says so.
  • IEA’s free Annex A dataset does not include Agriculture or Waste.

5. Indexing & interpolation

Because the sources cover different geographies (UK, France, World) and gases (CO2e vs. CO2), absolute levels cannot be compared directly. Comparisons therefore use an emissions index with 2025 = 100:

indext = 100 × Et / E2025

  • UK CCC has an observation for 2025. SNBC (2023, 2026, …) and IEA (2023, 2035, …) do not: their 2025 value is obtained by linear interpolation between the two surrounding points.
  • SNBC carbon budgets are annual averages over five-year periods; each is plotted at the midpoint of its period (2026, 2031, 2036), and the tooltip shows the period.
  • Series whose 2025 value is zero cannot be indexed and are hidden (the chart subtitle reports how many).
  • Lines between data points are straight; no smoothing or extrapolation is applied.

6. SBTi target trajectories

An SBTi target states a reduction r (share of base-year emissions) to be achieved by a target year. In the SBTi Targets view each target is turned into points of an emissions index with the base year = 100:

index(base year) = 100   ·   index(target year) = 100 × (1 − r)

  • Targets sharing the same company, scope and base year are stitched into one line (base year, then targets in chronological order). Targets with different base years are drawn as separate lines.
  • Only rows with a base year, a target year and r ≤ 1 are plotted; every matching row is listed in the table, where the wording is the SBTi target wording (or the full target language when missing).
  • The default filter shows Absolute targets. Intensity targets can be added, but their reduction refers to an intensity metric (e.g. tCO2e per unit of output), not absolute emissions.
  • SBTi does not prescribe a linear path between base and target year: the straight lines are a visual simplification.

7. SBTi target distribution vs. sector pathways

The Net-zero Pathway view summarises all relevant corporate targets of a sector and overlays them on the macro pathways.

  1. Eligible targets: SBTi sectors mapped to the selected harmonised sector (and subsector, if any); type Absolute; commitment status Active, Target set or not reported; selected scopes; base year ≤ 2025 and target year ≥ 2026; reduction, base year and target year all reported.
  2. Per-target path: a straight line from 100 at the base year to 100 × (1 − r) at the target year, evaluated each year from 2023 to 2050. No extrapolation: a target contributes only between its base year and its target year.
  3. Re-indexing: each path is divided by its own 2025 value (× 100) so that all targets — whatever their base year — start from 100 in 2025, like the macro pathways.
  4. Aggregation: for every year, the 25th percentile, median and 75th percentile of the re-indexed values are computed across targets (linear-interpolation quantiles, R’s default type 7). The shaded band is the interquartile range.

Points to keep in mind: each target counts once, so a company with several absolute targets (e.g. scopes 1+2 and 3) weighs more; the set of targets changes over time because short-dated targets drop out after their target year (most targets end in 2030–2035), so the band after ~2035 rests on fewer, longer-term targets; companies are not weighted by their emissions.

Highlighted companies are drawn with the same rule, as one line per company × scope × base year joining all its target points. The tooltip shows the reduction relative to the base year (Target) and relative to 2025 (Index). Find company jumps to the company’s most frequent SBTi sector, ticks the scopes it has targets for and highlights it.

8. On-track assessment (French companies)

The French Companies view compares what French corporates committed to (SBTi) with what they disclosed (NZDPU).

  1. Target selection: SBTi targets with location France, organisation type Corporate and type Absolute (intensity targets cannot be compared with absolute disclosures).
  2. Company matching, in order of priority: (a) exact LEI; (b) exact normalised name (lower case, punctuation removed, whitespace squished); (c) a manually curated list (inputs/manual_matches.csv).
  3. Scope alignment: NZDPU columns are summed to match each SBTi scope boundary — scope 1; scope 2 location-based (used by default); scope 3 total; and their combinations (1+2, 1+3, 2+3, 1+2+3). A missing component makes the sum missing.
  4. Base emissions: the NZDPU value disclosed for the target’s base year. Targets whose base year is not disclosed have no trajectory and no on-track status.
  5. Target path: linear from 100 at the base year to 100 × (1 − r) at the target year (flat at 100 before the base year and at the final level after the target year).
  6. On track in a given reporting year when the emissions index (disclosed / base × 100) is ≤ the target path index.

The headline percentage and the sector chart use, for every target (SBTi ID × scope × target year), its most recent reporting year (targets whose latest year has no status are left out), and count targets, not companies. Sectors are NZDPU’s SICS sectors. Unmatched companies are listed in outputs/review/ for manual review.

9. Limitations

  • Different perimeters: UK CCC and SNBC are territorial national inventories; IEA covers world energy-related CO2; SBTi targets cover a company’s global operations and, for scope 3, its value chain. Indexed comparisons show relative ambition, not equivalent quantities.
  • Gases and sources: IEA values are CO2 from fuel combustion only (Mt CO2); others are all GHG (MtCO2e). IEA “Energy” corresponds to power-sector inputs.
  • Linear paths: straight lines between base and target years (and between sparse scenario points) are a simplification of real trajectories.
  • Sector mapping is a judgement call: SBTi sectors are industry classifications (what a company sells), whereas national inventories classify where emissions occur.
  • Calendar vs. fiscal years are not distinguished. SBTi target values are used as reported; a single outlier (> 100 %) is kept but excluded from charts.
  • Disclosure coverage: NZDPU coverage is partial and recent (few 2023 disclosures at the time of extraction); scope 3 disclosures are often incomplete and methodologies change over time.
  • Snapshots: all datasets are static extracts; they are refreshed only when the pipeline is re-run.

10. Reproducibility

  1. Run the R processing scripts (R/sbti/process_sbti.R, R/uk_ccc/process_uk_ccc.R, R/snbc/process_snbc.R, R/iea/process_iea.R, R/nzdpu/fetch_nzdpu.R, then R/analysis/sbti_nzdpu_match.R) to rebuild data_prepared/*.rds.
  2. Run python scripts/export_web_data.py to export them to docs/data/*.json (sector mapping is read directly from R/utils/sector_mapping.R).
  3. Commit the updated docs/ folder: the site is static and is served as-is by GitHub Pages or Vercel.
